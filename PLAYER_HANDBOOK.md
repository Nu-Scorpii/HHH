# UA Hero College Player Handbook

Create your student hero and learn to play • Player edition

You play a student at UA Hero College in a My Hero Academia alternate universe. Your class trains, solves problems, protects people, and faces dangerous missions together. Your character is normally 18 or older. The Game Master, or GM, describes the world and plays the people you meet. You decide what your own hero says and does.

This handbook teaches character creation, Quirk creation, and every rule you need as a player. Begin with Create your student, then follow the creation sections in order. The later sections explain play and give examples. Keep the quick reference at the end beside your character card during sessions.

## Start here

The most important rule is **describe your goal first**. You can say, "I want to hold the door open so everyone can escape." You do not have to know the name of a game action yet. The GM helps you choose the right rule.

Your character has four stats: **Power, Speed, Wits, and Heart**. Each stat is a die. A d8 is a die numbered from 1 to 8. Roll the chosen stat's die and add nothing.

| Roll | Result |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | Miss or setback. |
| 4 to 7 | Success. |
| 8 or more | Big success with the action's extra benefit. |

You only roll when something is possible, risky, and uncertain. Everyday actions and harmless Quirk use usually happen without a roll.

**Edge** means roll two dice of your stat's size and keep the higher. **Pressure** means keep the lower. If you have both, roll one die. Extra sources do not stack.

You begin with **6 Health**, **3 Energy**, **two signature moves**, **one Talent**, and **one Plus Ultra use per session**. Health measures how much more you can take. Energy pays for enhanced moves. Your ordinary Quirk abilities work without Energy.

In a fight, you can **move one zone and take one action** on your turn. Your team chooses its turn order. Each hero acts once, then the enemies act. Being useful can mean attacking, helping, protecting, rescuing, or solving a problem.

You do not need to perform a character voice or give a speech. "My hero reassures the frightened student and shows them the exit" is enough to explain an action.

## Roll any dice

Type `/roll` to roll a d20, or choose your dice with `/roll dice:1d20`. The bot shows every die and a bold total. Add points with `/roll dice:1d20 bonus:3`; a roll of 8 becomes 11. Negative bonuses subtract points. You can put modifiers in the dice expression too, such as `/roll dice:2d17+1d8-3`.

For a simple best-of-two roll, choose `dice:2d20 keep:high`. `keep:low` keeps the lower die instead. Any modifier or bonus is added after the choice. Keep-high/low works on one positive numeric dice pool.

For weighted or text faces, use `/roll dice:1d[custom] faces:["sun","rain","rain","storm"]`. Repeated faces are more likely. Numeric custom faces, such as `[0,1,1,5]`, can use bonuses. Text faces show words and have no numeric total; they cannot use bonuses, modifiers, subtraction, or keep-high/low. Faces apply only to this roll.

Raw `/roll` results are useful for any extra dice the GM asks for. Tracked game actions use `/check` or the battle commands, with the stat rules above and their Accept/Plus Ultra buttons.

## Create your student

Follow these seven steps. You can change an unfinished choice while building your character.

**Step 1 Choose a hero idea.** Write one sentence about the kind of person you want to play. For example: "A careful ice sculptor who wants to make rescues safer." A brave fighter, fast rescuer, curious investigator, or encouraging teammate can all work. These are ideas, not classes with separate rules.

**Step 2 Choose a person.** Give them a real name, a hero name, an age that fits this college AU, and one or two personality traits. A long backstory is optional. You can discover more about them through play.

**Step 3 Give them a reason to be here.** Choose one goal, such as becoming a rescue professional, making a difficult Quirk useful, or earning the confidence of their family. Add a reason they cooperate with classmates. You can have disagreements while still helping the team.

**Step 4 Assign your stats.** Place d10, d8, d8, and d6 among Power, Speed, Wits, and Heart. Use each starting die exactly once. The next section helps you choose.

**Step 5 Create your Quirk.** Describe one power, its ordinary uses, its reach, and one or two clear limits. Use the Quirk sections to turn your idea into a short card.

**Step 6 Choose two signature moves and one Talent.** Pick move templates that fit your Quirk, then rename them to match your hero. Your Talent gives Edge on one suitable roll per scene. You start knowing two moves and one Talent.

**Step 7 Finish your card.** Set Health to 6 out of 6, Energy to 3 out of 3, Plus Ultra to ready, and Training to 0. Start with a costume, communicator, and sensible ordinary equipment. Your starting special support item is None.

Send your card to the GM before your first mission. Agree on your Quirk's uses, reach, and limits, and make sure its two moves fit. If you are joining a campaign already in progress, ask how many shared stat increases the class has earned; you receive those increases too.

**Your creation checklist:** a person, a goal, four stat dice, one Quirk card, two move cards, one Talent, and your starting Health and Energy. The completed example later shows the whole result.

## Choose your stats

Put your d10 in an area you want your hero to be especially good at. The two d8s are other strengths. The d6 is an area where your hero needs more practice or help.

| Stat | What you use it for |
| --- | --- |
| Power | Lifting, hard hits, strength, and physically blocking danger. |
| Speed | Dodging, moving quickly, sneaking, and precise physical movements. |
| Wits | Clues, machines, plans, observation, and careful Quirk control. |
| Heart | Courage, persuasion, leadership, understanding feelings, and resisting mental pressure. |

**A d6 is still useful.** It can roll a normal success. It cannot naturally roll 8, even with Edge, so it cannot produce a big success on an ordinary check. Rolling the highest number on a die does not create a special result by itself. Plus Ultra can change your result to 8 when you need it.

Here are three possible starting assignments:

| Hero idea | Power | Speed | Wits | Heart |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Strong front line fighter | d10 | d8 | d6 | d8 |
| Fast rescue specialist | d6 | d10 | d8 | d8 |
| Careful problem solver | d8 | d6 | d10 | d8 |

You can choose any legal assignment. A friendly leader could put d10 in Heart instead. Your hero idea does not force a particular stat layout.

**Your Quirk does not always use the same stat.** Moving a heavy ice slab might use Power. Placing a precise ice foothold might use Speed. Working out how to safely freeze a broken machine might use Wits. Reassuring someone uses Heart, whether or not you have an ice Quirk.

Describe your method and agree on the stat before rolling. A good explanation needs to fit the actual action. Simply calling an ordinary punch clever does not automatically make it a Wits roll.

You can Attack without a damaging Quirk by using a normal physical method or suitable equipment. You can also Help, Guard, and work on objectives regardless of your Quirk. You always have useful choices beyond your biggest die.

## Build your Quirk

A Quirk tells us **what your hero can attempt**. Your stats tell us how well those attempts work under pressure. Your Quirk does not need its own stat or a separate damage calculation.

**Step 1 Pick one main power.** Start with a sentence such as "I can create and shape ice nearby" or "My arms produce adhesive ribbons." A single clear theme makes it easier to invent useful actions during play.

**Step 2 Give it ordinary uses.** Write two to four things your hero can usually do. Try to include a contribution to a fight and a contribution to rescue, movement, or investigation.

For an ice Quirk, ordinary uses might be making small cover, freezing a simple mechanism, building a short ramp, and firing an ice attack. For a ribbon Quirk, they might be swinging, pulling objects, tying things together, and attacking at range.

**Step 3 State its reach.** Touch powers and ordinary punches work in your own zone. A suitable ranged Quirk normally reaches your own zone or one connected zone when you can see a clear path. Write that reach into your card. Ask the GM before assuming your power reaches across the whole scene.

**Step 4 Write its boundaries.** Say what your starting power cannot do, then give it one or two clear limits. The next section shows the difference between a hard restriction and a weakness that gives Pressure.

**Step 5 Pick two enhanced uses.** Choose signature move templates that match your power. For example, an ice wall uses Shield, a stronger ice strike uses Heavy Hit, and an ice bridge that helps an evacuation uses Rescue Boost.

Name your Quirk and write the finished description in this form:

```text
QUIRK NAME: Cold Sculptor
ABILITY: I create and shape modest amounts of ice nearby.
ORDINARY USES: Cover, short ramps, freezing simple mechanisms,
and ice attacks into my zone or one connected zone.
CANNOT: Control all the weather or create an ice city at once.
LIMITS: Ice needs a nearby surface or a clear path from my body.
Intense heat gives Pressure when I use it.
SIGNATURE MOVES: Ice Wall = Shield; Frozen Bridge = Rescue Boost.
```

**An ordinary use is free; an enhanced effect uses its move card.** A small ice barrier can contribute to ordinary Guard. Shield spends Energy to protect several friendly targets against one attack. Describing the same barrier as enormous does not give the Shield effect for free.

New creative uses are welcome. Explain your goal and how your Quirk helps. The GM matches the attempt to an ordinary action or an appropriate move. A good roll improves an allowed action; it does not add an unrelated new power to your Quirk.

## Give your Quirk clear limits

Write limits that are easy to recognize during a scene. "I need an anchor to swing" gives everyone something clear to picture. "Sometimes my power is bad" does not explain when it matters.

| Type of limit | Example and effect |
| --- | --- |
| Hard restriction | Ribbon swinging needs an anchor. Without one, you need another route. |
| Hard restriction | A healing power requires touch. Get into the person's zone before using it. |
| Weakness | Oil makes ribbon adhesion unreliable. An affected sticking attempt has Pressure. |
| Weakness | Intense heat makes ice control difficult. An affected ice roll has Pressure. |

Agree on these details while creating the character. A hard restriction can prevent a particular attempt. A softer weakness changes the roll by adding Pressure. Neither one secretly changes the normal Energy price of your move.

**Keep your starting power useful in one immediate scene.** "I can lift a nearby portable object" gives you many useful ideas. "I can control every object in the city" needs a smaller starting version. If you like a huge power concept, describe the limited version your student currently has and leave room to grow.

Some power ideas need a little extra care:

- **Flight and teleportation:** Your power can give you a route other people cannot use. You still follow zone movement and turn limits. Rapid Move is an enhanced way to travel farther in a turn.
- **Transformations and mutant bodies:** Ordinary traits are part of your Quirk's description. Use your stats and normal rules for attacks and defense. A tough-looking body does not automatically avoid every hit.
- **Clones, companions, drones, or summoned objects:** They act as part of your one turn. More bodies do not give you more separate turns.
- **Copied powers:** Agree on what you can copy. Copying changes allowed descriptions or permissions; it does not add Energy, extra actions, or extra prepared moves.
- **Healing:** Free uses can soothe or stabilize someone. Restoring Health during a fight uses Mend or an approved healing item.
- **Time, mind, or power control:** Give these a narrow starting scope. A Quirk does not automatically win a scene or take away another player's choices.

If you are unsure, send the GM a short power sentence and a few uses before writing a long backstory. You can work out boundaries together and then finish your card.

## Borrow a Quirk idea

You may use these ideas or adapt them. The suggested signature moves still use the normal move cards.

**Blast Palms.** Make short explosive bursts from your hands. Attack into a connected zone, propel yourself along a clear route, or break weak debris. Your hands must be free; saturated conditions give Pressure on explosive uses. Try Heavy Hit as Focused Blast and Burst as Scatter Blast. This is a good fit for a hero who likes strong hits and crowd clearing.

**Ribbon Arms.** Make adhesive ribbons that pull, tie, swing, and attack into a connected zone. Swinging needs an anchor; oil gives Pressure on adhesion. You cannot lift enormous loads. Try Capture as Ribbon Bind and Rapid Move as Swing Rescue. This hero can interrupt danger and move people to safety.

**Echo Voice.** Direct sound to signal, distract, detect nearby movement, or make ranged sound attacks into a connected zone. You must be able to vocalize. Soundproof barriers block the effect; overwhelming competing noise gives Pressure. Try Burst as Echo Wave and Long Reach as Focused Note. Use ordinary Heart checks to coordinate people and Wits checks to understand echoes.

**Light Touch.** Change the weight of one touched person or portable object enough to carry, guide, or reposition it. You must touch the target first. The power does not launch people into space or pass them through sealed rooms. Unwilling enemies still need an appropriate action or move. Try Rescue Boost as Floating Evacuation and Capture as Heavy Step.

**Mending Hands.** Help people recover through touch. Comfort and stabilization are ordinary uses; restoring combat Health uses Mend. You cannot reverse death, regrow body parts, or refill someone else's Energy. Try Mend as Recovery Touch and Rescue Boost as Guided Recovery. You can still Attack, Help, and Guard while saving Energy for a crucial heal.

**Cold Sculptor**, shown in the creation example, can emphasize protection with Shield and Rescue Boost, or stronger offense with Heavy Hit and Burst. One theme can support several styles of hero. Your two prepared moves identify which enhanced tricks you bring to a mission.

## Choose your signature moves

Pick **two** move templates that fit your approved Quirk. Name each one and keep its rule written beside the name. You start knowing both and prepare both for your first mission.

| Move | Energy cost | What it is good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy Hit | 1 | More damage against one enemy. |
| Burst | 1 | Smaller hits against several nearby enemies. |
| Rescue Boost | 1 | Faster progress on a suitable task. |
| Shield | 1 | Protection against one attack, including a group attack. |
| Capture | 1 | Briefly restraining or interrupting an enemy. |
| Rapid Move | 1 | Traveling farther while keeping an ordinary action. |
| Long Reach | 1 | Attacking a visible enemy beyond normal range. |
| Mend | 2 | Healing or reviving someone in your zone. |

The next two sections contain the full move rules. Read the cards for your two choices now. You can return to the others when you unlock more options.

**Pay Energy before using a move.** Subtract its price from your current Energy. A miss still spends it. You need enough Energy to pay the cost; your maximum is 3. At 0 Energy, your ordinary actions and basic Quirk uses still work.

**Use at most one signature move on your turn.** Most replace your action with an enhanced action. Rapid Move replaces your free movement and leaves you an ordinary action. It cannot combine with another signature move on the same turn.

You activate these moves on your own turn. Shield is protection you prepare in advance; it is not an extra reaction you buy after an enemy has attacked.

An approved move must fit your Quirk. An ordinary ice Quirk cannot take Mend just by calling it Healing Ice. Mend requires a healing Quirk or a specifically approved support concept.

After unlocking more moves, choose only two to prepare before each mission. Owning an unprepared move does not let you use its enhanced effect during that mission. Your ordinary Quirk abilities remain available.

## Attack and control move cards

**Heavy Hit costs 1 Energy.** Use your action to Attack one enemy within normal reach. Roll the appropriate stat. On 4 to 7, deal **3 damage**. On 8 or more, deal **4 damage**. On a miss, deal no damage. Help can add 1 damage to a hit and bypass Armor.

Choose Heavy Hit when one target needs a stronger hit. A focused explosion, heavy ice hammer, or reinforced punch can all use this card.

**Burst costs 1 Energy.** Use your action to Attack up to **three enemies together in one zone** within normal reach. Name the targets before rolling, then roll once. On 4 to 7, deal **1 damage to each**. On 8 or more, deal **2 damage to each**. On a miss, hit none.

Apply Armor to each target separately. If any selected target involves Pressure, that Pressure applies to the whole roll. Help gives Edge to the roll, but its extra damage and Armor bypass apply to only **one target chosen before rolling**. Check whether your Quirk can safely avoid friendly people nearby; any collateral risk is explained before you commit.

Choose Burst when several weaker enemies are grouped together. It is not three full ordinary attacks.

**Capture costs 1 Energy.** Use your action to restrain one enemy within normal reach and roll a suitable stat. On 4 to 7, the enemy cannot move until its next action ends, and that action deals **1 less damage** if it attacks. On 8 or more, it **loses that next action entirely**. On a miss, you do not restrain it.

The effect ends after that action; an unused effect ends after the next enemy phase. Multiple Captures do not stack; keep the stronger effect. A boss can lose at most **one action in a round**. If it has another action, that other action can still happen.

Capture can stop a dangerous attack or interrupt an enemy doing something important. It is a brief restraint, not permanent control of a conscious major enemy. Once an enemy is defeated at 0 Health, you can secure it without another roll if you have the means.

**Long Reach costs 1 Energy.** Use your action to make an ordinary Attack against one **visible enemy anywhere in the scene**, using a ranged power that can plausibly reach them. On 4 to 7, deal **2 damage**. On 8 or more, deal **3 damage**. On a miss, deal none.

A blocked line of sight still prevents the attack. Choose Long Reach when reaching the target matters more than increasing your damage.

## Rescue and support move cards

**Rescue Boost costs 1 Energy.** Use your action on a rescue, repair, evacuation, or other progress task your Quirk can enhance. Roll an appropriate stat. On 4 to 7, add **2 progress**. On 8 or more, add **3 progress**. On a miss, add none unless the GM had offered success with a stated cost.

Describe how your Quirk helps. An ice ramp might speed an evacuation; ribbons might lift debris. You still need any required first step, such as opening a blocked route.

**Shield costs 1 Energy.** Use your action to prepare protection in your zone. No roll is needed. Reduce damage from **one enemy attack by 2** for every friendly target of that attack in the protected zone. This can protect several people from a group attack.

It expires at the start of your next turn and is spent on the first attack that would damage someone it protects. It protects against one attack, not every attack in the enemy phase. Shield does not stack with Guard or another Shield on the same target; apply one protection effect. If damage becomes 0, the protected person does not roll defense or gain bonus defense movement.

**Rapid Move costs 1 Energy.** Before your action, move to **any reachable zone in the current scene**. This replaces your normal free move. You can carry one cooperative person if your Quirk allows it. Then take one ordinary action, such as Attack, Help, Guard, or work on an objective.

You cannot use another signature move on this turn. A sealed barrier still needs a way through, and moving a trapped person may require freeing them first. No roll is required for the move's normal effect.

**Mend costs 2 Energy.** Use your action to heal one person in your zone. No roll is needed. Restore **2 Health**, up to their maximum of 6. If they are Down, bring them back at **1 Health** instead.

Each hero can benefit from only **one healing action per fight**, counting Mend and Medical Patch together. Someone healed and then knocked Down again cannot receive another healing action in that fight. Mend requires a healing Quirk or an approved support concept. Free ordinary healing uses soothe or stabilize people without restoring Health during a fight.

## Choose your Talent and starting gear

Choose **one Talent** from this list. It gives Edge on one qualifying roll per scene. Declare it **before** rolling, then mark it used. A new scene refreshes it. Edge never stacks, so keep your Talent for another roll if you already have useful Edge and do not need to spend it.

| Talent | The roll it can improve |
| --- | --- |
| Heavy Hitter | One Power roll. |
| Quick on Your Feet | One Speed roll. |
| Sharp Thinker | One Wits roll. |
| Strong Spirit | One Heart roll. |
| Rescue Specialist | One roll directly saving or protecting someone. |
| Quirk Specialist | One roll using a prepared signature move. |

Pick a Talent you expect to use. If both your signature moves work without a roll, Quirk Specialist cannot improve those automatic effects. A stat Talent or Rescue Specialist may suit you better.

**Starting equipment** includes your costume, communicator, and sensible ordinary objects. Describe them briefly. These do not have prices or automatic stat bonuses. Special support equipment is unlocked with Training later; your starting special item is None.

A costume can explain how your hero carries things or uses their power safely. An ordinary costume feature does not give extra actions, free healing, or the mechanical effect of an enhanced item.

Before submitting your card, check these details:

- Your stats use d10, d8, d8, and d6, with no extra starting dice.
- Your Quirk has a clear theme, ordinary uses, reach, and understandable limits.
- Both signature moves fit that Quirk or an approved support concept.
- Each move card names its template and correct Energy cost.
- Your Talent says which roll it improves and that it is once per scene.
- Your Health is 6, Energy is 3, and Plus Ultra is ready.
- You have a goal and a reason to work with your classmates.

Agree on uncertain Quirk details with the GM before the first mission. You do not need to memorize other players' moves to begin.

## Read a finished character

Mira is a careful student who likes designing escape routes. She has a strong planning stat, an ice Quirk, and two rescue and protection moves.

For a careful bridge-building rescue, she might use Wits with Rescue Boost and spend her Talent before that roll.

You can copy this card and change its details, or fill in the blank card near the end. This example starts in a new campaign with no shared stat increases yet.

## Use rolls during play

Use this order whenever you need a roll:

**First, say your goal and method.** "I use ice to hold the broken lift in place while the passengers escape."

**Next, confirm the stat, risk, and effect.** The GM identifies the stat and any Pressure, and explains the consequence of a miss. Confirm whether you are using an ordinary action or a signature move.

**Then, declare costs and bonuses.** Spend any required Energy. Decide whether to use your Talent, Prep, or an item before rolling. Name Burst targets and its assisted target before the roll too.

**Roll and keep the correct result.** Use one stat die normally, two keeping higher with Edge, or two keeping lower with Pressure. If both apply, use one die.

**Finally, resolve the result and update your card.** You may choose Plus Ultra after seeing the roll. Apply the action's success or big success, then any relevant Health cost. Mark Help, Talents, Prep, or items used as appropriate.

Examples with Wits d10:

- Normal roll: 5. You succeed.
- Edge: 2 and 8. Keep 8 for a big success.
- Pressure: 9 and 3. Keep 3 for a miss or setback.
- Help and Pressure together: roll one d10. Help's attack damage bonus and Armor bypass still apply if this is an assisted attack that hits.

Several sources of Edge do not produce more dice. Several sources of Pressure do not produce more dice either. Any Edge and any Pressure cancel, regardless of how many sources you have.

On an ordinary attack miss, you do not deal damage. Enemies still get their announced turns; the miss itself does not give them an additional attack. Other risky actions may lose time, attract attention, or have another stated consequence. A roll of 1 has no extra universal fumble penalty.

If an attempt fails, change your approach, get new help, or accept a new cost before repeating it. For a shared task, your group can choose one lead roller and have others contribute through Help.

## Move and take a turn

A **zone** is a named area, such as Street, Roof, or Lobby. The GM tells you which areas connect. Within your zone, you can normally get close enough to act unless something blocks the way.

Your turn gives you **one free move and one action**. A clear free move crosses one connected zone. Move before or after your action; you do not split it into several trips. You may stay where you are. Ordinary movement does not trigger opportunity attacks.

Punches and touch powers reach your own zone. Suitable ranged powers or weapons can reach your zone or one connected zone with a clear line of sight. Long Reach or an agreed Quirk permission handles greater distance.

You can carry or guide one cooperative person with your move if your hero is capable of doing so. A trapped, frightened, or blocked person might need an action first. Moving someone does not automatically finish an evacuation objective.

| Action | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Attack | Roll. On 4 to 7, deal 2 damage. On 8 or more, deal 3. |
| Help | Give one teammate a benefit on a named task; see the next section. Usually no roll. |
| Guard | Protect yourself or someone in your zone from one attack; see the next section. No roll. |
| Do something | Work on a rescue, device, conversation, or other useful goal. Roll if risky. |
| Recover | Restore 1 Energy, up to 3. No roll. |
| Rush | Use your action for one additional clear zone move. A dangerous route may need a check. |

Most signature moves are enhanced versions of these actions. You still receive your normal free movement, except Rapid Move replaces it.

A **round** begins with announced enemy intentions. Your team chooses an order, and each hero takes one turn. Then enemies act. Enemies do not make attack rolls; their targets make defense rolls. A defeated enemy loses its actions. Many enemies act once; a boss may act twice.

Enemy intentions can change if their target becomes unreachable or the situation changes. Moving one zone is not guaranteed to stop an enemy that can follow you. Listen for reach, movement, target, and damage when a threat is announced.

## Use Help Guard and Armor

**Help** uses your action. Name one teammate, one task, and how you contribute: "I distract the bruiser so Ren can land his next hit." It normally needs no roll, but you must be able to contribute. If reaching a helpful position requires a risky feat, that feat can take this turn's action first.

Your teammate's **next matching roll before the start of your next turn** gets Edge. If it is an attack, one successful hit also deals **1 extra damage and ignores Armor**. The Help is spent on that matching roll even if it misses. You cannot Help yourself. A hero can hold only one Help; additional Helps do not stack.

You can Help a defense, rescue, or investigation too. Those get Edge, but the damage bonus only applies to an attack. For Burst, choose one target before rolling to receive extra damage and Armor bypass; Edge applies to the whole roll.

**Guard** also uses your action. Choose yourself or one person in your zone. Before your next turn, reduce damage from **one attack against that person by 2**, to a minimum of 0. No roll is needed to prepare Guard.

Apply the reduction before a defense roll. An attack already at 0 damage before protection does not spend Guard or Shield. Guard is spent when a damaging attack resolves, even if the defense succeeds. If damage becomes 0, skip defense; there is no big defense result and no bonus movement. Multiple Guards do not stack. Shield follows the same no-stacking rule for protection on one target.

For example, Guard reduces a 3-damage attack to 1. A successful defense then avoids that 1 damage. A failed defense takes 1. Against a 2-damage attack, Guard reduces it to 0 and protects the target without a roll.

**Armor** reduces damage from each unassisted hit by **1**, to a minimum of 0. An ordinary 2-damage hit therefore deals 1 against Armor. An assisted ordinary hit deals 3 and bypasses Armor. A discovered and actually exposed weak point may remove Armor, but merely saying "I hit a weak point" does not create one.

Help can also cancel Pressure from a protective enemy or device. Solving the situation that creates Pressure can remove it for the whole team. Extra sources of Edge cannot overpower Pressure by stacking.

Choose Help when a teammate's next roll matters. Choose Guard when preventing damage from an announced attack matters. If you want to act later in the team order to support someone next round, remember when your Help or Guard expires.

## Defend and use Plus Ultra

When an attack targets you, describe how you defend. Use a sensible stat: Power for a physical block, Speed for a dodge, Wits for a real prepared countermeasure, or Heart against a mental effect. Heart does not automatically block a normal punch. Agree on the method and stat before rolling.

| Defense result | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 3 | Take the attack's damage after protection reductions. |
| 4 to 7 | Avoid the attack completely. |
| 8 or more | Avoid it and optionally move one zone after it resolves. |

If an attack hits several heroes, each targeted hero defends separately. Everyone resolves that attack before bonus movement happens. Damaging hazards can use the same defense rules.

**Health and being Down.** Start with 6 Health. Subtract damage from your current Health, stopping at 0. At 0 you are Down and cannot take normal actions. On one remaining turn in that fight, you can cheer or call useful advice to give one normal Help. You can do this **once per fight**, even if you later recover and go Down again.

Mend can bring a Down hero back at 1 Health, subject to the limit of one healing action per recipient per fight. A Medical Patch cannot revive you. After the fight, basic assistance can wake a Down hero at 1 Health without otherwise healing damage. Being Down does not automatically mean death.

**Safe recovery.** Ten safe minutes restores all your Health and Energy. Without that safe time, your current amounts carry into the next encounter. During a fight, Recover restores 1 Energy as an action; it does not restore Health. Ordinary Quirk use remains available when your Energy is 0.

**Plus Ultra** is a special effort you can use **once per session**. After seeing one of your rolled results, change it to **8**. Resolve the big success, then lose **2 Health**. It can improve an action or a defense, including under Pressure. Guard and Shield do not reduce this Health cost.

Original Energy costs still apply. Plus Ultra cannot buy a move you lack Energy to use or make an impossible attempt possible. You must have made a valid roll; an automatic action such as ordinary Guard has no result to change.

If you have 2 Health left, you can still use Plus Ultra. Complete the action's big success, then fall to 0 and become Down. That can be a heroic choice when the action will save someone.

## Play missions and smaller scenes

Listen for the mission's goal. Defeating enemies may matter, but rescuing people, stopping a machine, escaping, or protecting an object can be the real objective.

A task may have a **progress track**, such as Evacuation 2 out of 4. A normal successful action adds **1 progress**; a big success adds **2**. Rescue Boost adds **2 or 3** instead. Ask what counts toward the goal and whether another step is required first.

For example, opening a blocked route might be necessary before evacuation progress begins. Carrying someone along a clear route can help, but the announced task determines how progress is earned.

A **Danger track** shows time or rising risk. It normally gains one mark at round end unless you stop its cause. When it fills, the announced consequence happens. Your failed roll does not automatically add an extra mark unless that risk was stated first.

Ordinary conversations, classes, investigations, and social scenes use the same four stats when a roll is needed. Background interests explain what you know or try; they do not require a second list of skill scores. You can describe what you say instead of acting out every word.

In a joint problem, choose a lead roller and useful supporting roles. Help can be as valuable in an investigation as in a fight. A good question, clue, or established relationship can make a later choice easier.

**Friendly player sparring** uses a short contest. Play best of three exchanges; the first player to win two wins the spar. Each exchange, describe your approach and roll an appropriate stat. The higher kept result wins, even if both numbers are below 4. Exact ties are rerolled without paying costs again.

Before an exchange, you may spend 1 Energy to gain Edge through an enhanced Quirk use. Talents and valid Prep can also apply, with the usual no-stacking rule. This contest uses no Health damage, prepared move effects, Help damage, or Plus Ultra. Winning does not earn extra Training. A tactical training fight against a GM-controlled opponent uses ordinary combat instead.

## Earn Training and prepare for missions

**Participating earns Training.** Each official session gives you **1 Training stamp**, up to **2 per server week**. Main missions, sparring, support labs, investigations, and smaller character scenes count equally. Check that a scene is an official session and which weekly reset your server uses.

A useful decision, supporting contribution, or thoughtful character moment can count as meaningful participation. You do not need to speak the most or win. Failed training rolls still count. Repeated trivial checks, kills, and unapproved chat grinding do not award extra stamps.

Spend **3 Training** during downtime to unlock **one** new signature move, Talent, or special support item. Subtract the spent stamps from your stored total and record the new option. You keep unlocked options.

For a mission, prepare **two known moves, one known Talent, and at most one special support item**. Choose during downtime before the mission. Learning a third move gives you a choice of which two to prepare; it does not give you three active move slots.

**Side sessions also give Prep.** Record one theme linked to the activity, such as rescue practice or rival research. It grants **Edge on one relevant roll in the next main mission**, declared before rolling. Hold only one Prep. If you earn another, choose which to keep. Unused Prep expires when that next main mission ends. It does not stack with other Edge.

For example, rescue practice earns 1 Training if you are below the weekly cap, plus one rescue Prep. You can use that Prep on a later rescue roll. Research may also reveal a useful fact; a social scene may establish a relationship or favor.

**Shared milestones improve stats.** When the class earns a shared stat increase, raise one of your dice by one step: **d6 to d8, d8 to d10, or d10 to d12**. The maximum is d12. Mark the number of shared increases received. Everyone gets the same number, including returning and new players. Health remains 6 and maximum Energy remains 3.

A major milestone may also expand an agreed Quirk permission, such as carrying heavier loads or reaching a new kind of route. Record the approved change. It does not automatically give extra turns or free signature move effects.

You can miss a session and return at the class's shared stat progress. Your Training unlocks and current mission preparation are recorded separately.

## Use special support equipment

You unlock a special support item by spending 3 Training. Prepare at most **one** for a mission. Each item is usable **once per scene**, with the extra healing restriction on Medical Patch. Ordinary equipment stays available, but does not copy these enhanced effects for free.

**Grapple Line.** Use your free move to cross one gap or climb one difficult connection with a suitable anchor. No action or roll is needed for that crossing. It does not add a second move or cross a sealed wall.

**Smoke Capsule.** Before a roll, give yourself or one teammate Edge on one escape or hiding roll. It does not add attack damage. Mark the item used when you use it.

**Rescue Harness.** For one move, carry or guide two cooperative people instead of one, provided you could normally move at least one of them. Free or calm them first if required.

**Field Scanner.** Spend your action to learn one useful true fact about a nearby enemy, hazard, or device. Ask a specific question, such as what powers its shield or which route is safest. No roll is needed. The scanner works only within its agreed scope.

**Medical Patch.** Spend your action to restore **1 Health** to someone in your zone who is above 0 Health. It cannot revive a Down hero. Each person can receive only one healing action per fight, counting Medical Patch and Mend together. Outside combat, the item's once-per-scene limit still applies.

Mark your prepared item's use on your card. You can still investigate or attempt a route without the matching item; describe your ordinary method and use the normal rules.

## Know when things refresh

Some resources refresh at different times. A **scene** is one meaningful encounter or challenge, such as a fight, rescue, chase, or important conversation. A **session** is a GM-declared play session. A **mission** can contain several scenes or sessions. Listen for the GM to announce their boundaries.

| What you track | When it refreshes or ends |
| --- | --- |
| Health | Ten safe minutes restores it fully to 6. Healing may restore less. |
| Energy | Recover adds 1 during your turn; ten safe minutes restores it to 3. |
| Talent | Ready again when a new scene begins. |
| Special item | Ready again when a new scene begins; its other limits still apply. |
| Plus Ultra | Ready again at the start of a new GM-declared session. |
| Healing received and Down cheer | Clear these fight-use marks at the start of a new fight. |
| Help received | Spent on the named roll, or expires at the helper's next turn. |
| Guard or Shield | Spent on the protected attack, or expires at the user's next turn. |
| Prep | Spent on one relevant roll, or expires at the next main mission's end. |
| Weekly Training allowance | Resets each server week. Stored unspent Training is kept. |
| Prepared options | You may change them during downtime between missions. |

Walking into a new room or taking a short break does not automatically create a new scene or session. A new scene alone does not refill Health or Energy. A new session refreshes Plus Ultra; Health and Energy still need the normal recovery rules.

Only mark what currently matters. During a fight, that usually means Health, Energy, Plus Ultra, your Talent, your item, and any Help or protection. Training is updated after sessions, and prepared choices are selected between missions.

## Watch one round of play

Mira, Ren, Juno, and Sol face an armored Bruiser with 6 Health and a Drone with 2 Health. The civilians need 4 evacuation progress through an open doorway. The Bruiser intends to punch Ren for 2 damage; the Drone intends to shoot Juno for 1. The team chooses its order.

**Mira takes her turn first and Helps Ren.** She makes the floor slippery beneath the Bruiser to create an opening. This uses her action, needs no roll, and spends no Energy. Ren's next attack gains Edge, 1 extra damage on a hit, and Armor bypass.

**Ren uses Heavy Hit.** He spends 1 Energy, going from 3 to 2. His Power is d10. With Edge he rolls 3 and 8, keeping 8. That is a big success. Heavy Hit deals 4 damage and Help adds 1, for **5 damage**. The hit ignores Armor. The Bruiser falls from 6 Health to 1.

**Juno makes an ordinary ribbon attack on the Drone.** Her precise shot uses Speed d10. She rolls 5, a normal success, and deals 2 damage. The Drone reaches 0 Health and is defeated. It will not make its announced attack.

**Sol works on evacuation.** He uses Heart d10 to get the frightened civilians moving. He rolls 8, a big success, and adds 2 progress. The goal is now 2 out of 4. He did not use Rescue Boost, so he spends no Energy.

**The remaining enemy acts.** Ren defends against the Bruiser with Speed d8 and rolls 2. He takes 2 damage and goes from 6 Health to 4. If he had received Guard, that 2-damage attack would have been reduced to 0 without a defense roll.

At round end, any announced Danger track advances as normal. In the next round, one ordinary successful hit can finish the Bruiser: 2 damage minus 1 Armor deals its last 1 Health. Other heroes can work on the remaining evacuation progress.

Notice the different useful choices. Mira improved a teammate's action. Ren spent Energy on a stronger hit. Juno removed a threat before it acted. Sol advanced the actual mission. All four heroes contributed.

## Make smart choices

You can begin by choosing a goal and rolling your die. As you learn, these habits help you get more from a turn.

**Plan the team order.** Ask who needs an opening or protection. A helper can act before an attacker. A rescuer may need someone to clear the route first. Each person still gets only one turn.

**Match the move to the problem.** Heavy Hit is a stronger hit against one enemy. Burst is several smaller hits against a group. Long Reach solves distance. Rescue Boost solves progress. Spending Energy is useful when its enhanced effect matters.

**Use Help when your own attack is less useful.** Helping a strong attacker, a pressured teammate, or someone facing Armor can be a good contribution. Against an unprotected target, two strong attackers may prefer to attack separately.

**Stop announced threats.** Finishing a weakened enemy removes its action. Capture can interrupt an important action. Guard can prevent damage from a known attack. A boss may still act again after one action is cancelled.

**Change the situation.** Open a safe route, remove a protective device, or expose a real weak point. Removing the cause of Pressure can help several heroes. Fancy wording alone does not create Edge.

**Save limited benefits for different rolls.** Help, your Talent, an item, and Prep may all grant Edge, but they do not stack. If one is enough, save the others. Declare optional benefits before rolling.

**Keep ordinary actions in mind.** You can attack normally, Help, Guard, and work on objectives at 0 Energy. Recover is useful when you need another enhanced move, but it uses your action.

**Choose Plus Ultra for a result that matters.** A guaranteed big success might save a person or finish a threat. Consider whether losing 2 Health afterward will leave you Down.

**Share useful information.** A clue from a side session, a Quirk limit, or a prepared move can affect the group's plan. Explain what your hero can contribute and let teammates choose their own actions.

## Answers to common player questions

**Do I need a damaging Quirk to fight?** No. Ordinary physical attacks and appropriate equipment use the usual Attack rules. You can also Help, Guard, or complete objectives.

**Does every Quirk attack cost Energy?** No. Ordinary Quirk use, including an ordinary Attack, is free. Your enhanced signature move costs what its card says.

**Can I invent a new use of my Quirk?** Yes. Describe the goal and method, then agree on an ordinary action or suitable move. A creative idea still follows reach, limits, costs, and turn rules.

**Does an 8 let me hit twice?** No. A big success gives the benefit written for that action. It does not add a second full action. Rolling a die's maximum is not automatically a big success unless the kept result is at least 8.

**Can I spend my Talent after a bad roll?** No. Talents, Prep, and optional item bonuses are declared before rolling. Plus Ultra is the option you can choose afterward.

**Can I use Rapid Move and Heavy Hit together?** No. Rapid Move is your one signature move for that turn. You can move and then make an ordinary Attack, Help, Guard, or another ordinary action.

**Can I use a third signature move I learned?** Only if you prepared it as one of your two for this mission. Record learned options separately from prepared options.

**Does a new session refill Health and Energy?** Not by itself. Plus Ultra refreshes with a session. Full Health and Energy recovery requires ten safe minutes; Recover restores 1 Energy as an action.

**Can several teammates Help me to cancel several Pressures?** One source of Edge already cancels any Pressure under this system. Extra Helps do not stack. If you remove the actual cause of Pressure, another source of Edge can then work normally.

**What if I am quiet or need to step away?** You can participate with short descriptions and useful choices. For text play, agree on a standing instruction such as Guard or Help a named teammate. Energy and Plus Ultra should only be spent for you with your standing permission. A skipped turn does not automatically remove your session credit.

## Keep this quick reference

**Roll your stat die and add nothing.** 1 to 3 misses, 4 to 7 succeeds, and 8 or more is a big success. Edge rolls two and keeps higher; Pressure keeps lower. Any Edge and any Pressure cancel. Extra sources do not stack.

**Your turn:** move one connected zone and take one action. The team chooses order, each hero acts once, then enemies act. Pay signature costs before using them; at most one signature move per turn.

| Ordinary action | Quick rule |
| --- | --- |
| Attack | Success deals 2; big success deals 3. |
| Help | Name ally and task. Next matching roll before your next turn gets Edge. Attack hits also gain 1 damage and ignore Armor. |
| Guard | Yourself or one person in your zone. Reduce one attack by 2 before your next turn. No roll to prepare. |
| Do something | Describe goal. A progress success adds 1; a big success adds 2. |
| Recover | Restore 1 Energy, maximum 3. |
| Rush | Use action for one extra clear zone move. |

**Defense:** 1 to 3 takes damage, 4 or more avoids it, 8 or more also allows one zone move. Guard reduces damage first; if it becomes 0, skip the roll and bonus movement. Armor reduces unassisted hits by 1.

**At 0 Health:** Down. No normal actions. Cheer to give normal Help once that fight. Healing is limited to one healing action per recipient per fight; Mend can revive at 1, Medical Patch cannot.

**Plus Ultra:** once per session, change one of your rolled results to 8. Resolve it, then lose 2 Health. Original Energy costs still apply; it can leave you Down.

**Refreshes:** ten safe minutes restores Health and Energy. Talents and special items refresh each new scene. Plus Ultra refreshes each new session. A new scene or session alone does not refill Health or Energy.

**Growth:** 1 Training per official participated session, maximum 2 per server week. Spend 3 on one move, Talent, or item. Prepare two moves, one Talent, and at most one special item per mission. A side session also gives one themed Prep for Edge on one relevant roll in the next main mission. Hold only one; it expires at that mission's end. Shared stat increases go to the whole roster.
